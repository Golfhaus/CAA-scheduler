### 526 — strong hub extension

DAL 04:58 → MCI 06:25; MCI 07:15 → DAL 08:42. DAL–MCI increases from **two to three flights daily each direction**; the prior DAL departures were 10:05 and 18:30. The morning inbound reaches MCI's early connection waves. The outbound to DAL draws arrivals in the morning MCI bank. Connecting opportunity dominates the new legs while the local market also contributes about 34 allocated points each way.

Flight **1206 DAL–PHF moves from 09:20 CT–13:13 ET to 09:32 CT–13:25 ET**, a 12-minute shift. Every later 526 flight retains its clock: PHF–DAL 15:25 ET–17:18 CT; DAL–BHM 18:10–19:51 CT; BHM–DAL 20:35–22:16 CT. The new turn has **50 minutes at MCI and 50 at DAL** before the original flight.

The shift loses **zero existing modeled connection choices on Flight 1206**, adds one, and creates no slower best itineraries or lost markets. Its connecting opportunity changes from about 262.9 to 266.1 in the standalone comparison. A demand-maximizing +10-minute variant departs DAL 05:16, returns 08:50 and yields about 469/253 opportunity, but uses two exact 40-minute turns. The buffered choice scores about 383/261 and provides more recovery margin. An unchanged-clock variant also fits with exact 40-minute turns and about 392/263 opportunity.

DAL–MSY is a credible alternative: roughly 132/131 daily local O-D, with no modeled connecting contribution and no existing nonstop. A buffered 04:52 DAL departure/07:06 MSY return can preserve every old clock. It is saved in the broader comparison. For this request's hub/frequency strategy, the underserved morning MCI coverage is the selected option.

## Holistic checks and search limits

All three unchanged midday holds are too short for even the shortest complete out-and-back after accounting for the 40-minute turns before, between and after the new legs: 526's PHF hold is 132 minutes, 163's JAX hold 105, and 509's BHM hold 123. Separate earlier/later clock rebuilds were examined. A valid larger PHF hold on 526 fails pairing spacing downstream at DAL–BHM and gate inventory on the top tested loops; other nominal cascades collide with existing DAL–PHF or SAT–MCI departures inside the hard 30-minute floor.

The screen is bounded, not a proof of a global schedule optimum. Short windows use five-minute timing grids plus endpoints. Fresh wide-hold screens use a 15-minute grid and one-stop marginal ranking (earlier dense-grid results are retained in the checkpoint), followed by full nonstop/one-stop/two-stop demand allocation on retained choices. Up to two top timings per market receive complete physical validation in the wide screen. The model never bypasses failed passenger gates, bank assignments, curfews, pairing floors or continuity to make an option look viable.
