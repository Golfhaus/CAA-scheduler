# Schedule 7 v1.1.4 tactical moves

Schedule 7 v1.1.4 was developed as a three-round cumulative feasibility series
and released after Round 3. Round 1 implements the reciprocal PHF/FLL originator
suggestion without increasing the MAX9 aircraft-day count or exceeding the
six-flight directional market ceiling.

- Add a 05:00 FLL-PHF positioning flight. After an 82-minute turn, that aircraft
  operates F1106 PHF-FLL and the former Route 716 suffix.
- Add a 06:05 PHF-FLL positioning flight. After a 42-minute turn, that aircraft

  remains at 21 aircraft-days.
- Replace Route 703's 07:00 PHF-FLL nonstop with PHF-BHM-FLL, preserving the

improves from 9 stand claims and 5,126 minutes to 8 claims and 4,287 minutes.

## Generalized tactics

### Reciprocal pre-originator exchange

When two aircraft begin late at opposite ends of the same market, add reciprocal
early positioning legs and let each aircraft assume the other's downstream
work. Validate minimum turns, directional frequency ceilings, hub-bank timing,
gate peaks, and cycle closure together; checking only the two new legs misses
the principal constraints.

### Cap-neutral path conversion

If the exchange would create a seventh daily nonstop, convert one existing
flight in each direction into a useful two-leg path. The intermediate city must
have frequency headroom and enough block-time slack to preserve the downstream
route. Round 1 uses BHM on Route 703 and SFB on Route 705.

### Overnight bridge

An early terminator can support a late flight back to the next day's originator
base, followed by an early out-and-back that restores the original sequence.
This consumes daytime and overnight slack without adding an aircraft-day.

### Gate-wave relief by surgical retiming

When a new originator creates one excess passenger touch, move the least
connection-sensitive departure just beyond the peak and retime only the minimum
number of following legs needed to protect legal turns and bank membership.
Round 1 resolves FLL's fifth simultaneous touch with an 18-minute maximum shift
on Route 706.

### Reciprocal secondary-hub extension

When two spoke cities finish early after service to their primary nearby hubs,
use the remaining day to visit the other city's hub, return to the spoke, and
then add one more round trip to the primary hub. This increases cross-hub access
while preserving the next-day aircraft position.

### Bank-edge spillover at a saturated hub

If a hub has no legal gate slot for another minimum-time turn, preserve the
existing bank connections by placing the new arrival just after the gate peak
and documenting a narrow exception for the return departure. This is preferable
to pulling an existing departure forward when that change would break inbound
connections. The exception must still pass curfew, spacing, and fixed-inventory
checks.

Five aircraft that previously terminated at MCI between 14:00 and 16:59 now
operate demand-backed out-and-backs and return for the late banks:

- Route 133 adds MCI-CLT 14:45-18:04 and CLT-MCI 18:44-20:03. This is a
  third round trip in a 62.5-passenger average daily O&D market.
- Route 135 adds MCI-PNS 15:35-17:45 and PNS-MCI 18:25-20:35. This is a
  third, well-spaced round trip in a 23.4-passenger market.
- Route 116 adds new nonstop service with MCI-CAK 15:37-18:42 and CAK-MCI
  19:40-20:45. The 18-minute-longer CAK turn staggers the arrival into Bank 11
  and avoids a temporary seventeenth MCI gate requirement.
- Route 152 adds MCI-IND 16:25-18:55 and IND-MCI 19:35-20:05. This is a
  second round trip in a 37.8-passenger market.
- Route 344 adds MCI-MKE 18:05-19:30 and MKE-MCI 20:10-21:35. This is a
  fourth round trip in a 28.8-passenger market and productively uses the
  route's later timing.

All five aircraft turn from their MCI arrival gates, return within Banks 10 or
11, and remain at gates for their RONs. The round adds ten legs without adding
an aircraft-day or operating exception. The cumulative candidate has 1,022
legs, 179 routes, and 23 rotation lines, with zero effective operating errors.
Stand use improves from 29 claims and 16,607 minutes after Round 2 to 24 claims
and 12,888 minutes; MCI falls from five stand claims to zero.

### Bank-tail utilization

An aircraft terminating into a mid-afternoon hub bank can operate one more
out-and-back when its destination block time is matched to a late return bank.
The useful range varies by arrival time: earlier terminators can reach longer
markets, while offset aircraft should serve shorter markets. Returning after
the final large departure wave can convert a stand RON into an on-gate RON.

### Frequency-grid market assignment

Rank candidate markets by demand and service deficiency, then test each against
its existing departure grid. The highest-demand choice is not automatically the
best one: a new departure can fail Section 2.6 when an existing frequency is too
close. Assigning different markets to several available aircraft produces more
useful coverage than stacking flights into one crowded market.

### Staggered late-bank returns

When several added round trips return together, use destination ground-time
slack to move one arrival into the next bank. Round 3 holds Route 116 at CAK for
58 minutes rather than 40, moving its MCI arrival from 20:27 to 20:45 and
eliminating the only seventeenth-gate conflict.

## Release

The released v1.1.4 schedule is defined by the three tactical overlays in order.
It contains 1,022 legs across 179 routes and 23 rotation lines, retains the
approved BHM connection model, and passes structural, operating hard-stop, and
planning-reconstruction validation with zero effective operating errors. It is
the default schedule in the application.
