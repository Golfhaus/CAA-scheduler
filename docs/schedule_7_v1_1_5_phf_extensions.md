## Generalized tactics

### Pull a mini-bank toward newly available aircraft

When several aircraft become available immediately after a major bank, move an
underused mini-bank within its existing 60-minute policy width rather than
creating a new bank or adding exceptions. PHF-M6 moves from 19:45-20:05 to
18:52-19:52, retaining its existing arrivals while accepting the new outbound
extensions.

### Use an early terminator twice

An especially early aircraft can support two separate round trips when the
first return is aligned with the next departure opportunity. Route 718 adds a
JAX round trip, returns at 18:26, and turns in 40 minutes for PIT. This provides
more useful flying than choosing one market and leaving a multi-hour PHF hold.

### Move unavoidable ground time away from the constrained station

If the last legal return bank is later than a simple minimum-time turn, hold the
aircraft at the outstation when that station has physical capacity. Route 354's
RFD hold permits a 22:14 PHF return without creating another RFD stand claim,
replacing a much longer PHF stand hold.

### Return after the departure wave

Aim the extensions at the late-arrival mini-bank after the final large PHF
departure wave. This allows arriving aircraft to occupy gates for their RONs
instead of being towed away to protect the 20:55 departures.

## Round 2 generalized tactics

### Split a late terminator across two operating days

Send the aircraft out in the late bank, RON it at a close spoke, and attach the
return to the following route in the same rotation line. This preserves line
continuity while turning an otherwise idle overnight into a first-bank inbound.

### Protect maintenance-base RONs with productive returns

When a line depends on the hub for its rolling maintenance-base RON, use the
aircraft for a curfew-compatible round trip that returns into the new late bank
instead of moving the overnight away from the base.

### Treat the following departure as the limiting constraint

An outstation RON is useful only when its 04:30 return leaves a legal aircraft
turn before the route's existing first departure. Small retimes make three
continuations work; Route 518 is deliberately left unchanged because forcing
its much earlier continuation would require a broader route ripple.

## Round 3 generalized tactic

### Move non-connecting departures to release a saturated-bank gate

A departure at the front of a bank may appear useful but still leave before
the bank's inbound passengers can make the minimum connection. Moving that
flight slightly earlier can release a gate without destroying a usable
connection. A later new-bank frequency can then preserve service for the
inbound wave.

## Round 4 generalized tactics

### Use alternating banks for multiple round trips

A long hub hold can support more than one round trip when each return reaches
the bank immediately preceding the next departure bank. Route 158 uses Banks
5, 7, and 9 while retaining a legal turn into its existing Bank 10 departure.

### Place the residual hold at the less-constrained station

When a route cannot fit another complete round trip, delay its outstation
return so the remaining ground time occurs away from the saturated hub. Route
309 holds 99 minutes at RFD and only 100 minutes at MCI after returning.
